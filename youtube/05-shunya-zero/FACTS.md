# Facts — शून्य (zero)
- Roman numerals have no symbol for zero; 2026 = MMXXVI.
- 628 CE: Brahmagupta's Brāhmasphuṭasiddhānta gives rules for arithmetic with zero (a + 0 = a, a − 0 = a, a × 0 = 0) — zero treated as a number.
- 876 CE: the Chaturbhuj temple inscription in Gwalior Fort writes "270" (and "50") with a round zero — among the oldest dated written zeros in India, still on the wall.
- The numerals reached Baghdad (al-Khwarizmi, c. 825, wrote on Hindu reckoning) and from there Europe.
- 1202: Fibonacci's Liber Abaci taught the Hindu-Arabic numerals to Europe.
- Wording note: other cultures (Babylon, Maya) had zero-like placeholders; the script says India made zero *a number*.
